The short version

Three paragraphs  ·  the facts are in the panel on the right
Robotics got me started. Security kept me curious. Machine learning is where I’ve ended up.

I’m reading Computer Science at KNUST in Kumasi — first year of four, finishing in 2029. I speak English, and enough French to hold a conversation. I got here through robotics, where I spent secondary school tuning a line-following robot until it won nationals, and through cybersecurity, where I ran the club and taught seven juniors. Both turned out to be the same job: watch what the system does, change one thing, watch again.

I’d rather work on problems that are close to home. Ghana has no shortage of them — farms that could use a better guess at next season’s yield, clinics deciding who to see first, power that comes and goes. It makes more sense to build for that here than to wait for someone somewhere else to get round to it.

When I’m not at a keyboard I’m usually reading, working through maths problems because I find them fun, or helping younger students get started in robotics and security.
